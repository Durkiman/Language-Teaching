Warm-up
Quick Fire: Must, Need To, Ought To
One sentence per prompt, straight away — no pausing to plan. Every prompt is a real moment from a client discussion.
Rapid-fire prompts
1
must
Say something a salesperson must always do when a client hesitates.
2
have to
Say something you have to check before you send a client a final contract.
3
need to
Say something you need to find out about a client's budget before you quote a price.
4
needn't
Say something a client needn't worry about once they've signed.
5
ought to
Say what you ought to do before you name a final price out loud.
6
don't have to
Say something you don't have to do to win this client's business.
🧑‍🏫 Teacher note: Keep this brisk — one sentence, immediate correction if needed, move on. The goal is re-surfacing B1-19's modal set (must/have to, need to/needn't, ought to) before today's client-discussion skin. Don't let any single prompt run long.

💡 Key idea: A client discussion isn't a fight to win — it's problem, explanation, proposal, resolution. Disagreement gets softened ("I see what you mean, but…", "Well, actually…"), never dropped.

Free Production · Roleplay
Negotiate the Deal
Two client discussions. For each: state the problem, explain why it matters, propose a resolution, respond to pushback — softening disagreement where it fits.
Client · Teacher plays this Discussion 1 — Price Pushback
"Your proposal looks solid, but the total investment is more than we allocated for this quarter. I need to see this cost differently before I can move forward."
🧑‍🏫 Push back once more after the first response — ask "so are you saying it should just be cheaper?" and make him hold his position without simply lowering the price.
Mehdi · Consultant Your Task — 4 Steps
  • 1. Understand — ask what "more than budgeted" really means for them this quarter.
  • 2. Explain — say why the price reflects the scope, using need to / have to.
  • 3. Propose — offer a resolution: a phased start, a smaller scope, a payment plan — using ought to.
  • 4. Respond to pushback — hold your position calmly, softening with "I see what you mean, but…" rather than backing down.
Client · Teacher plays this Discussion 2 — Delivery / Timing Change
"The three-month timeline works for me, but starting next Monday doesn't — I need at least three more weeks before we can kick off."
🧑‍🏫 A calmer objection than Discussion 1 — good contrast. Let him practice proposing a middle-ground date rather than just accepting the delay outright.
Mehdi · Consultant Your Task — Same 4 Steps
  • 1. Understand — what's really behind needing three more weeks?
  • 2. Explain — why a long delay could affect the results or the schedule.
  • 3. Propose — a middle-ground start date, not the full three-week delay.
  • 4. Respond to pushback — accommodate without losing momentum on the project.
Phrases to Use — Tap When Used
