Warm-up
Quick Fire: Must, Have To, Need To
One sentence per prompt, straight away — no pausing to plan. Every prompt is a real moment from setting a price or a meeting time with a client.
Rapid-fire prompts
1
must
Say something a consultant must always do before naming a price out loud.
2
have to
Say something you have to check before you confirm a meeting time.
3
need to
Say something you need to find out about a client's schedule before proposing a time.
4
needn't
Say something a client needn't worry about once a price is agreed.
5
don't have to
Say something you don't have to do to close this deal.
🧑‍🏫 Teacher note: Keep this brisk — one sentence, immediate correction if needed, move on. Re-surface must / have to / need to / needn't only — no "ought to" today, it's being retired from Mehdi's active modal set as too formal/archaic for spoken use. If it slips out, gently redirect to "should" or "the best move is to…" instead.

💡 Key idea: Price and time are separate negotiations that often move together — flexibility on one (payment plan) can offset firmness on the other (fixed scope). Propose a number confidently, then invite pushback rather than avoiding it.

Free Production · Live Negotiation
Set the Price. Set the Time.
Two full negotiations, back to back. For each: propose a number/date, justify it, invite pushback, then hold firm or adjust — using must / have to / need to / needn't / don't have to. No "ought to."
Client · Teacher plays this Negotiation 1 — Price
"Before we go further — what's this actually going to cost me, and can we do better on the number?"
🧑‍🏫 Let him propose a real number first. Push back once ("that's still more than I hoped") and make him hold the price while offering flexibility elsewhere (payment terms, phased scope) instead of just discounting.
Mehdi · Consultant Your Task — 4 Steps
  • 1. Propose — name a specific price out loud, with confidence.
  • 2. Justify — say why it's set at that level, using have to / need to.
  • 3. Invite pushback — ask directly if the number works for them.
  • 4. Hold or adjust — if they push back, offer flexibility on structure (payment plan, phasing) using don't have to / needn't — without simply lowering the price.
Client · Teacher plays this Negotiation 2 — Meeting Time
"Okay, I think we're close on price. Now — when can we actually meet to sign this off?"
🧑‍🏫 Give him a real scheduling conflict once his first date is proposed ("that week doesn't work for me at all"), so he has to genuinely re-propose rather than just repeat the first offer.
Mehdi · Consultant Your Task — Same 4 Steps
  • 1. Propose — a specific day and time.
  • 2. Justify — briefly say why, using need to / have to (checking calendar, respecting a deadline).
  • 3. Invite pushback — ask if that time actually works.
  • 4. Hold or adjust — if it doesn't, propose a genuine alternative using needn't to show what doesn't need deciding yet (e.g. exact hour).
