Belgium in Six Facts

Read each card out loud, then react to it in your own words before moving to the next.
Languages
Belgium has three official languages: Dutch (Flanders, north), French (Wallonia, south), and German (a small eastern region). Brussels is officially bilingual (Dutch/French).
Food
Famous for fries (eaten with mayonnaise, not ketchup), waffles, chocolate, and beer — Belgium has over 1,500 beer varieties and a centuries-old brewing tradition, including Trappist monastery beers.
Directness
Belgians tend to be polite but fairly direct and understated — modesty is valued, and loud self-promotion is often seen as impolite.
Punctuality & small talk
Being on time matters, especially in Flanders. Small talk before business is common but usually brief — weather, travel, or a quick personal check-in.
Design & architecture
Belgium has a strong design and architecture scene — Art Nouveau started partly in Brussels (Victor Horta), and Belgian designers are internationally respected in fashion and interiors.
Regional identity
Flemish (Dutch-speaking) and Walloon (French-speaking) communities have distinct identities, and regional pride is common — it's not one uniform "Belgian culture."

Giving an Opinion With a Reason

Tap a card to reveal how to use it. Say each one out loud with a real opinion about Belgium.
In my opinion, ... because ...▾
The basic claim + reason shape. Always add "because" — a bare opinion sounds unfinished at B1.
"In my opinion, Belgian design culture is really strong because they take craftsmanship seriously."
What surprises me most is...▾
Great for reacting to new cultural facts — sounds natural and personal.
"What surprises me most is that Belgium has three official languages — that's very different from Korea."
One thing I'd like to experience is...▾
Ties opinion to a future goal — useful when talking about studying abroad.
"One thing I'd like to experience is the local design scene, especially in Brussels or Antwerp."
Compared to Korea, ... seems ...▾
Comparative structure for culture-contrast talk.
"Compared to Korea, small talk in Belgium seems much shorter and more to the point."
