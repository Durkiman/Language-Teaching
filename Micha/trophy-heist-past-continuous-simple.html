Quick Review

Petit rappel

was/were + verb-ing = the long action (the alibi)
past simple = the short, sudden event
An alibi is a long action interrupted by a short event:
"I was watching the cameras when the lights went out."
🔎 Detective tip: A good alibi uses was/were + -ing for what the suspect claims they were doing. Evidence and facts use past simple — real actions that really happened. If a suspect's "-ing" story doesn't match the past simple facts... someone is lying!

Un bon alibi utilise "was/were + -ing" pour l'action prétendue. Les preuves utilisent le passé simple — des faits réels. Si l'histoire ne correspond pas aux faits... quelqu'un ment !
