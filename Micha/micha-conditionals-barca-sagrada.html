Barcelona's two unfinished icons

Les deux icônes inachevées de Barcelone

Barcelona has two very famous places. They're both huge, both loved by millions of visitors — and both are still under construction, even today!

⚽

FC Barcelona: the Blaugrana

FC Barcelona — everyone calls it Barça — is one of the most famous football clubs in the world. Fans founded the club in 1899, and the players still wear the same colours today: blue and dark red. People call these colours "blaugrana". Barça's stadium, Camp Nou, is currently being rebuilt. Right now it holds about 62,000 fans, but soon it will hold 105,000 — more than any other stadium in Europe!

Le Barça (les couleurs "blaugrana" = bleu et grenat) joue au Camp Nou, actuellement en reconstruction.

⛪

La Sagrada Família: Gaudí's masterpiece

The architect Antoni Gaudí started building this enormous church in 1882 — more than 140 years ago! In February 2026, workers finally finished the highest tower, the Tower of Jesus Christ. At 172.5 metres, La Sagrada Família is now the tallest church in the world. But it still isn't 100% finished: some parts, like the Glory Façade, won't be ready until around 2034.

L'architecte Gaudí a commencé la Sagrada Família en 1882. La tour centrale est enfin terminée (2026), mais le bâtiment entier ne sera pas fini avant 2034 environ.

Teacher note A2-19 core teaching. Zero conditional = general truths/facts (present + present). First conditional = real future consequences (present + will). Both texts give natural examples: "If you visit Camp Nou now, you see building work" (zero, still true today) vs "If Camp Nou finishes in 2027, it will be the biggest stadium in Europe" (first, a real future result). Watch for the classic mistake: "will" in the if-clause.

Zero & first conditional

Conditionnel zéro et premier conditionnel

If + present simple, ... present simple
(a fact — always true)
  • If you visit the Sagrada Família today, you see cranes and building work.
  • If it's hot in Barcelona, people go to the beach.
If + present simple, ... will + verb
(a likely future result)
  • If Barça win on Saturday, the fans will celebrate on La Rambla.
  • If Camp Nou finishes in 2027, it will be the biggest stadium in Europe.
Common mistake: Don't use "will" after "if". ✗ If you will go to Barcelona... → ✓ If you go to Barcelona, you will...

Après "if", on n'utilise jamais "will" — seulement le présent simple.
