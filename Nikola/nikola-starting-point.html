    Goodbye, shared e-scooters

    Read the article, then answer the questions below it.

    Shared e-scooters arrived in Brussels in 2018. The idea was simple: you opened an app, found a scooter near you, rode it across the city and left it almost anywhere. A lot of people loved it. In 2025, nearly 800,000 people used them for almost 10 million trips.

    But the problems grew fast. Scooters were left in the middle of pavements, and the number of accidents went up. In 2025, 666 people were injured in e-scooter accidents in Brussels, 26% more than the year before. When riders fall, they are more likely than cyclists to hurt their head or face. The city tried drop-off zones, an automatic speed limit (20 km/h everywhere, 8 km/h in the pedestrian zone) and an identity check in the app. None of it really solved the problem.

    In June 2026, the Brussels government decided to ban shared e-scooters from 1 January 2027. In the end, they disappeared even sooner, and not because of the accidents. Back in 2023, the city had decided to allow only two scooter operators, Bolt and Dott. Lime, a company that did not get a licence, took that decision to court. This summer, the Council of State agreed with Lime. However, it did not give Lime a licence. Instead, it cancelled the licences of the other operators, and they were told to take their scooters off the streets by 1 September.

    Not everyone is happy. Bolt says its scooters had become an essential part of how people get around the city, and that people often confuse them with private e-scooters, which have no company checking them. Some experts also ask who loses the most. Studies show that people who use shared scooters are, on average, slightly less educated and less likely to have a driving licence than people who use shared bikes. That is why one mobility professor called the ban "not socially neutral". Brussels is not alone: Paris, Madrid and Prague have already removed their shared scooters.

    Based on reporting by The Brussels Times (August 2026) and Euronews (June 2026).
