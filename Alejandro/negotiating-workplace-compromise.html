Negotiation exponents

Fixed phrases for proposing, conditionally accepting, and closing a compromise.

1What if we…?
2I could agree to that if…
3That works for me, as long as…
4Let's meet halfway
Drill the intonation — these need a softening, collaborative tone, not a flat delivery. Contrast "That works for me, as long as…" with a flat conditional to show how the exponent itself carries the negotiating register.

Gap-fill: conflicting priorities

Read the context, fill the blank with a negotiation exponent, then check.

Your client wants a lower price, but you can't cut the materials.
"______ we keep the current materials but adjust the timeline instead?"
What if we keep the current materials but adjust the timeline instead?
Your manager wants the report by Friday; you need more data first.
"I ______ agree to Friday ______ I get the numbers by Wednesday."
I could agree to Friday if I get the numbers by Wednesday.
Your team wants remote Fridays; the company wants full in-office days.
"That ______ for me, ______ we're all in for key meetings."
That works for me, as long as we're all in for key meetings.
You want a $10k budget for the project; finance offers $6k.
"______ — how about $8k?"
Let's meet halfway — how about $8k?

Sentence-building

Unscramble each set into one natural sentence.

if / the launch date / I / agree to / could / stays the same / the extra features / drop
I could agree to drop the extra features if the launch date stays the same.
we / cut the scope / what if / instead of / the deadline / moving
What if we cut the scope instead of moving the deadline?
If gap-fills go quickly, extend with two more oral prompts of your own (e.g. remote work days vs office presence, headcount vs budget) — same exponents, no script. Push for the conditional clause to be complete, not just the fixed opener.
