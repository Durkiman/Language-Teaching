Common patterns — quick reference

Spanish-L1 pronunciation patterns
Final consonants — dropped or softened (final -s, -d, -t). Contrast minimal pairs: "cats" vs "cat".
/b/–/v/ merge — both realized close to Spanish "b". Contrast: "berry" vs "very".
Vowel reduction — unstressed vowels given full value instead of schwa. E.g. "photograph" vs "phoTOgraphy".
Word/sentence stress — even stress across syllables instead of one strong peak.
/h/ — often dropped or replaced with a Spanish "j" sound.

Can-do cards in play

B2-11
Interrupting, holding the floor & yielding it
Function: managing complex interaction.
Grammar: turn-taking phrases, hedging to hold the floor, signalling readiness to yield.
Evidence: handles at least one interruption without losing the thread, and interrupts back appropriately once.
B2-01
Extended narrative with evaluation
Function: narrating with evaluation and comment.
Grammar: past perfect continuous; full range of narrative tenses.
Evidence: tells a 2–3 minute story including a past perfect continuous clause and two evaluative comments.
B2-15
Linking ideas across a longer stretch
Function: structuring extended discourse.
Grammar: varied linking devices beyond and/but/because, sustained over several turns.
Evidence: speaks for an extended stretch with ideas clearly linked, not just listed.
B2-08 / B2-09
Hypothesising about present & future / Speculating about the past
Function: speculating and hypothesising.
Grammar: present/future conditionals; modal + perfect infinitive for past speculation.
Evidence: speculates naturally about an outcome — what he'd do, what might happen, what might have gone differently.
