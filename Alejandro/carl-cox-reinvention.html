Background: the reinvention

For the Reinvention discussion — a summary, not the source text, from a longer EDM.com feature.

Cox released his first live album, Electronic Generations, after decades known purely as a DJ. The idea had sat dormant since the start of his career — he'd always wanted to perform original material live, outside of DJ sets, but only acted on it after the pause forced by COVID-19. He now frames it as a shift in identity, not just a side project: performing as a live artist alongside his DJ work, rather than being defined by one role.

Discussing the article

Comprehension first, then his reaction.

  • Summarise what the residency actually involves, in your own words.
  • He says he wants his time in clubland to "leave a legacy." What do you think he means by that, specifically?
  • Does 16 dates over a season sound demanding to you, or fairly relaxed for someone at his level?
  • Would you go to a residency like this if you were in Ibiza? Why or why not?
  • What do you make of a 20,000-capacity club existing for this kind of music — does that surprise you?

Reinventing yourself

Carl Cox has been doing this for over 40 years — and recently repositioned himself from "DJ Carl Cox" to "Carl Cox, the artist," with his first live album. Use that as the hinge.

Reaction
  • What do you think it takes to stay relevant in a scene for four decades?
    • Personal stance
  • Have you ever had to reinvent something about how you work or what you do? What pushed you to do it?
    • Counterargument
  • Some would say reinventing yourself this late is a risk — you could alienate the people who liked you the way you were. Is that fair?
    • Hypothetical
  • If you'd built a reputation doing one thing for 40 years, what would make you change it — and what wouldn't?
    • Live "upgrade the sentence" targets to plant if the moment arises — precision register, not idiom:
    • reinvent yourself — vs. "change what you do"
    • stay relevant — vs. "still be liked"
    • double down on (something) — vs. "do more of"
    • come full circle — for the DJ→artist→DJ arc
    • push the boundaries of (something) — vs. "try new things"
    Background if he asks for more: Cox's first live album, Electronic Generations, marked a shift from DJing to performing original material as a live artist — his own words were "I'm not DJ Carl Cox anymore. I'm Carl Cox, the artist."
