Emotional Language

Feelings, Prepositions & Intensity

📘 1. Understanding the Rule

Talking about feelings needs three things: the right sentence pattern, the right preposition after the adjective, and the right intensifier for how strong the word already is.

feel/be + adjective
"I feel nervous." "She's thrilled."
feel like + noun/-ing
"I feel like a fraud." "I feel like crying."
it makes me feel + adjective
"It makes me feel anxious."
adjective + that-clause
"I'm worried that I'll forget."
Careful: "I feel bad," not "I feel badly." After feel/be, use an adjective (bad, sad, nervous), not an adverb — "badly" describes how you do an action, not how you feel.
🌡️ Gradable vs extreme adjectives

Gradable adjectives describe a feeling that comes in degrees (a little → very): angry, sad, tired, happy. Use very / really / quite / a bit.

Extreme adjectives already mean "very ___": furious (= very angry), devastated (= very sad), exhausted (= very tired), ecstatic (= very happy). Use absolutely / completely / utterly instead — never "very."

Careful: "very furious" is a mistake. Furious already means "very angry," so adding "very" doubles up. Say "absolutely furious" instead.
