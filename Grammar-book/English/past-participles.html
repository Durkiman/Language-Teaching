Past Participles

Present Perfect, Past Perfect & the Passive Voice

📘 1. Understanding the Rule

What is the past participle?

The past participle is the "third form" of a verb (base — past simple — past participle). For regular verbs it's identical to the past simple (work → worked → worked). For irregular verbs it's often a different word entirely (go → went → gone). For the full list of irregular forms, see the companion Irregular Verbs page — this page focuses on how and where the past participle is actually used.

Key confusion to avoid: the past participle is never used alone as a main verb tense — it always needs a helper (auxiliary) verb: have/has/had for perfect tenses, or be for the passive voice. "I have went" is wrong; "I have gone" is correct.

Where the past participle is used

✅ Present perfect
have/has + past participle
She has eaten.
🔙 Past perfect
had + past participle
They had left already.
🔁 Passive voice
be + past participle
The letter was written by Tom.
🎨 Adjective
describing a state or feeling
a broken window, a tired child
