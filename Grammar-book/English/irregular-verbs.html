Irregular Verbs

Base, Past Simple & Past Participle Forms

📘 1. Understanding the Rule

Three forms of every verb

Every English verb has three key forms: the base form, the past simple, and the past participle. Regular verbs follow a predictable rule — add -ed for both the past simple and the past participle. Irregular verbs don't: each of the three forms has to be learned individually, and the past simple and past participle are often different words.

Base Past Simple Past Participle
work (regular) worked worked (same form)
go (irregular) went gone (different form)
buy (irregular) bought bought (same form)
⚡ Three irregular patterns
All three the same

cut → cut → cut
put → put → put

Past & participle match

buy → bought → bought
think → thought → thought

All three different

go → went → gone
speak → spoke → spoken

Which form do I actually need?

✅ Base form
after did/didn't, modals, to
Did she go? / I can go.
🕓 Past simple
a finished action alone
She went home at six.
🔗 Past participle
only with have/has/had or be
She has gone home.
🚫 Never
past simple after did/have
"Did she went?" is wrong.
No shortcut: there is no reliable spelling rule for irregular forms — many past participles end in -en (eaten, broken, written) or -n (known, grown, flown), but plenty don't (bought, brought, made, said). They have to be memorised, ideally in the full base–past–participle triplet, not in isolation.

UseNote
