Trip Gone Wrong

Picking up the thread

Remember the trip we negotiated? Tell me where you ended up agreeing to go — then imagine you're already there.

Quick recall of the negotiation exponents from last time (What if we…?, Let's meet halfway) before the mood shifts — because on this trip, something has gone wrong.

This bridges from the 08-05 negotiation session. Get a one-line recap of what he agreed on with his travel partner, then pivot: "Great — now imagine you've just arrived and there's a problem." Keep it light, this is the hook into today's topic.

Opinion warm-up

Discussion opener
"What's the worst thing that's gone wrong on a trip for you, or someone you know?"
Follow-up
"In your opinion, what's the right way to complain when a service lets you down?"

Listen for B1-08 exponents (I think… / In my opinion…) appearing naturally — no need to name the target language.

Explaining a problem, requesting a fix

Fixed language for stating the problem, explaining its impact, and requesting resolution.

1The issue is that…
2This means we can't…
3What I'd like you to do is…
4I need this sorted out before…
Grammar focus for B1-19: must/have to, need to/needn't, ought to. Point out how these modals carry the "requesting resolution" force ("You need to move us to another room", "You ought to offer a refund") without sounding rude — contrast with a flat, unmarked statement.

Model call: hotel booking gone wrong

Read through once, then have Fayçal read the guest part while you take the hotel receptionist.

GuestHi, I'm calling about my reservation — I booked a room with a sea view, but you've put us in a room facing the car park.
ReceptionistI'm sorry about that, let me check. Yes, I see the booking — we're fully booked on sea view rooms tonight.
GuestThe issue is that I paid extra specifically for the sea view. This means we can't use what we already paid for.
ReceptionistI understand. What would you like us to do?
GuestWhat I'd like you to do is either upgrade us to a sea view room tomorrow or refund the difference. I need this sorted out before check-out.
ReceptionistThat's fair — I'll move you tomorrow and refund tonight's difference.

Gap-fill: service problems

Read the context, fill the blank with the right phrase, then check.

Your tour was cancelled with no warning and no alternative offered.
"______ the tour never happened, and I lost a full day of my trip."
The issue is that the tour never happened, and I lost a full day of my trip.
Your flight was changed to a day later, but you have a non-refundable hotel booked.
"This ______ make it to my hotel on the night I already paid for."
This means we can't make it to my hotel on the night I already paid for.
You want a concrete fix, not just an apology, from the airline.
"What I'd like you ______ is rebook us on the next available flight, free of charge."
What I'd like you to do is rebook us on the next available flight, free of charge.
You have a connecting flight and need an answer quickly.
"I ______ this sorted out before my connecting flight at 6pm."
I need this sorted out before my connecting flight at 6pm.

Sentence-building

Unscramble each set into one natural sentence.

is that / the issue / hasn't arrived / my luggage / and I have a meeting tomorrow morning
The issue is that my luggage hasn't arrived, and I have a meeting tomorrow morning.
ought to / a room / at least / offer us / for the inconvenience / you / free breakfast
You ought to at least offer us free breakfast for the inconvenience.
If these go quickly, extend orally with two more curveballs (double-booked activity, faulty rental car) — same exponents, no script. Push him to always follow "the issue is that…" with why it matters, not just what happened — that's the "explain why it matters" component of the evidence task.

Scenario 1 — wrong booking

Scenario: You arrive at "your" trip's accommodation and the reservation is wrong — wrong dates, wrong room type, or it's been given away. Call the provider (your teacher). State the problem, explain why it's a problem for you, propose a resolution, and respond to any pushback.
Evidence task — B1-19 (1 of 2)

Scenario 2 — faulty purchase / service

Scenario: Something you paid for on the trip doesn't work as promised — a tour that skipped half the sites, a rental car with a fault, or a spa treatment that was cut short. Call to complain: state the problem, explain the impact, propose a resolution, respond to pushback.
Evidence task — B1-19 (2 of 2)

If he gets talked over mid-explanation, that's the moment to nudge B2-11 incidentally — "hang on, let me finish" or "can I just come in here?" before continuing.

B1-19 — primary
Problems & non-routine situations
Function: explaining a problem; requesting resolution.
Grammar focus: must/have to, need to/needn't, ought to.
Lexis: travel and services vocabulary — check in, departure lounge, debit or credit card.
Evidence: two escalation roleplays (wrong booking; faulty purchase) — state the problem, explain why it matters, propose a resolution, respond to pushback.
B2-11 — incidental
Interrupting, holding the floor & yielding it
Function: managing turn-taking in group discussion.
Markers: can I just come in here, hang on — let me finish, go on, sorry — you were saying.
Touched only if the interlocutor talks over him mid-explanation during the roleplay.
B1-08 — incidental
Opinions with reasons
Function: expressing opinions.
Exponents: I think…, I don't think…, In my opinion…
Touched in the warm-up discussion about trip disasters and how to complain well — not the lesson's main focus.
