Trip Gone Wrong

Picking up the thread

Remember the Albania trip you were planning last time? Tell me what you were confident you'd do — then imagine you're already there.

Quick recall of the future-certainty language from last session (I'll definitely…, I'll probably…, I might…) before the mood shifts — because on this trip, something has gone wrong.

This bridges from lesson #2 (Albania — Culture & Future Plans). Get a one-line recap of one of his predictions for the trip, then pivot: "Great — now imagine you've just arrived and there's a problem." Keep it light, this is the hook into today's topic.

Opinion warm-up

Follow-up

Listen for B1-08 exponents (I think… / In my opinion…) appearing naturally — no need to name the target language.

Explaining a problem, requesting a fix

Fixed language for stating the problem, explaining its impact, and requesting resolution.

1The issue is that…
2This means we can't…
3What I'd like you to do is…
4I need this sorted out before…
Grammar focus for B1-19: must/have to, need to/needn't, ought to. Point out how these modals carry the "requesting resolution" force ("You need to move us to another room", "You ought to offer a refund") without sounding rude — contrast with a flat, unmarked statement.

Model call: hotel booking gone wrong

Read through once, then have Fayçal read the guest part while you take the hotel receptionist.

GuestHi, I'm calling about my reservation — I booked a room with a sea view, but you've put us in a room facing the car park.
ReceptionistI'm sorry about that, let me check. Yes, I see the booking — we're fully booked on sea view rooms tonight.
GuestThe issue is that I paid extra specifically for the sea view. This means we can't use what we already paid for.
ReceptionistI understand. What would you like us to do?
GuestWhat I'd like you to do is either upgrade us to a sea view room tomorrow or refund the difference. I need this sorted out before check-out.
ReceptionistThat's fair — I'll move you tomorrow and refund tonight's difference.

Reviewing what you've watched

A different skill, same theme: summarising a film or show and giving your opinion on it (B1-10). Travel-gone-wrong stories make good material either way.

1It was set in…
2It was about…
3It had a happy ending
4If you like X you'll like this
Model review — The Terminal (2004)
Tom Hanks · comedy-drama

It was set in a New York airport, almost entirely inside the terminal building. It was about a man whose passport stops being valid mid-flight because of a coup back home — so he can't enter the US, and can't fly home either. He ends up living in the terminal for months, dealing with one bureaucratic problem after another.

It had a happy ending — he finally gets to leave and do the one thing he came to New York for. If you like stories about someone calmly solving problem after problem, you'll like this.

Your turn: think of a film, show, or series where someone deals with a travel problem going wrong — a flight, a hotel, a lost passport, a strike. Give a short spoken review using the exponents above.

B1-10 incidental — don't push for the full 90-second evidence-task version today, just a natural minute of talk. If he draws a blank on travel-themed media, let him review anything he's watched recently; the exponents are the point, not the topic. Good bridge back into "and has anything like that ever nearly happened to you?" before moving to Practice.
