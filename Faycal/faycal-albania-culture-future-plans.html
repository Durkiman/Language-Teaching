Discussion openers

"What comes to mind when you hear 'Albania'? Have you ever met anyone from there?"
"If you were planning a trip somewhere completely new, how far ahead would you plan it — and how much would you leave open?"

📍 Where to go

Capital

Tirana

Apartment blocks painted in bold colour blocks — a former mayor's idea to shake off the grey Communist-era look. Bunk'Art turns a Cold War bunker into a museum.

UNESCO

Berat

The "City of a Thousand Windows" — Ottoman-era houses stacked up a hillside, castle at the top, river below.

UNESCO

Gjirokastër

The "Stone City" — grey stone roofs, a hilltop fortress, and the birthplace of writer Ismail Kadare.

Coast

Albanian Riviera

Ksamil and Sarandë — turquoise water, quieter and cheaper than Corfu, which you can actually see across the water.

One genuinely fun fact worth dropping in conversation: a head nod in Albania can traditionally mean "no," and a shake can mean "yes" — the reverse of most of Europe. Good icebreaker, not essential content.

📰 What's happening on the coast right now

Ongoing · since May 2026

Scenes from the "Flamingo Revolution" marches — inflatable flamingos, Albanian flags, and signs directed at the resort deal.

Not everything about the Riviera is postcard-perfect at the moment. Since May 2026, protests have been building in Zvërnec, a small village by the Narta Lagoon — right in the heart of the region. Local residents, environmental groups and ordinary Albanians are opposing a huge planned resort: around €5 billion, close to 10,000 rooms, backed partly by American investors. The problem is location — the site borders a protected wetland that's home to flamingos and nesting sea turtles.

Nicknamed the "Flamingo Revolution" — protesters carry inflatable flamingos at marches, echoing the real ones in the lagoon they're trying to protect.

Protesters say the approval process lacked transparency and that land ownership in the area is genuinely disputed. The government and developers point the other way — to the investment, jobs and tourism revenue a project this size could bring. Prime Minister Edi Rama has defended the plan and offered talks with a protester delegation, an offer rejected so far. Marches in Tirana have drawn crowds in the thousands, with one recurring chant: "Albania is not for sale." As of mid-2026, nothing is resolved.

Genuinely live and contested — keep your own framing neutral in the room. Good material for opinion language and today's predictions ("Do you think it will go ahead?"), but gauge his interest before spending much time on it; not everyone wants politics in a language lesson.

Planning the trip — a short text

I'm planning a trip to Albania this autumn, and I've already worked a few things out. I'll definitely start in Tirana — everyone says the colourful buildings and Bunk'Art are worth a full day. From there, I'll probably head south along the coast; Ksamil looks incredible in photos, though I might skip the busiest weeks in August. By the time I reach Berat, I'll be slowing down — this time next month, I'll be wandering through the old town with no real plan. One thing I've read: a head nod doesn't always mean what you'd expect here, so I'll definitely ask a local to explain it properly.

Three patterns, all doing the same job: being precise about how sure you actually are.

1 · Grading certainty (will + adverb, might/may)

will definitely / probably + verb  ·  might / may + verb (base)

"I'll definitely visit Berat." (certain)

"I'll probably skip Tirana's nightlife." (fairly confident)

"I might extend my trip by a few days." (genuinely unsure)

Fr : Je vais sûrement / certainement... · Il se peut que...

❌ "Maybe I will definitely go." — mixing two certainty levels in one clause.
✅ Pick one: "I'll definitely go" or "I might go."

2 · Going to vs will (quick recap)

going to = already decided  ·  will = opinion-based prediction, decided now

"I'm going to book my flights this week." (a firm decision already made)

"I think I'll enjoy the coast more than the cities." (an opinion, not a fact yet)

Fr : je vais + infinitif (décidé) vs je pense que + futur (opinion)

❌ "I go to visit Albania next year." — missing "am going to."
✅ "I'm going to visit Albania next year."

3 · Future continuous — in progress at a future moment

will be + verb-ing

"This time next month, I'll be exploring the Albanian Riviera."

"By 6pm, I'll still be driving through the mountains."

Fr : je serai en train de...

❌ "This time next month, I will explore..." — sounds like one finished action, not something ongoing at that moment.
✅ "I'll be exploring..."

This is the evidence task for today's can-do card — a "this time next year" monologue about a real or imagined trip to Albania.

The task

Imagine it's exactly one year from now. Describe your Albania trip — where you are, what you're doing, what's already happened. Aim for:

One certain prediction ("I'll definitely...")
One probable prediction ("I'll probably...")
One possible prediction ("I might...")
Two future continuous sentences ("I'll be...")
Tick these live as you hear them — don't announce the checklist to him beforehand beyond the general task. If he leans on "going to" throughout instead of grading certainty, that's the signal this needs a second pass next session.
