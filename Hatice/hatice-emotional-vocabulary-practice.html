📘 Grammar: Emotional Language

Emotional Vocabulary — Widening Your Bandwidth

Practice at your own pace, in your own time

The Bottleneck

There is so much inside you that wants to be expressed. But when you're speaking a foreign language, that language has a narrower bandwidth than your first language — so even when the feeling is big and clear inside you, it gets squeezed through a smaller opening. That's the bottleneck.

The good news: every word you learn widens the bottleneck. The more precise your emotional vocabulary, the more of yourself you can actually get through — and the more confident and complete you'll feel speaking English.

This isn't about learning "happy" and "sad." It's about learning the shades in between — because your inner life already has those shades. English just needs the words to catch up.

How These Words Are Organized

Each emotion family has three levels of intensity:
Soft
A quieter, early version of the feeling.
Medium
The feeling in its clear, obvious form.
Intense
The feeling at its strongest.
