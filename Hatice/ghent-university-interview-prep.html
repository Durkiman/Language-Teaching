Facts Worth Naming in the Room

Generic praise ("it's a great university") sounds the same from every candidate. These specifics let her sound like she actually researched the place.
Identity
Founded 1817 — the first Dutch-speaking university in Belgium. Motto: "Durf Denken" ("Dare to Think"). A pluralist university: not tied to any religion or ideology, open to all.
International profile
~41,000 students, over 6,000 international students from 130+ countries — a strongly international, multilingual environment.
Standing
QS World Rankings 2026: #162 globally, up from #169 the year before. #2 in Belgium, just behind KU Leuven.
Research reputation
Affiliated with several major scientists, including Peter Piot (co-discoverer of the Ebola virus, global HIV/AIDS researcher) and Marc Van Montagu (plant biotechnology pioneer) — a strong evidence-based research culture.
Where dentistry sits
Dentistry is part of the Faculty of Medicine and Health Sciences, spread across 10 departments — dental training is embedded in a larger medical/clinical research environment, not siloed.
Dental specialty strength
Ghent has a well-regarded, EFP-accredited postgraduate program in Periodontology & Oral Implantology — clinically oriented, evidence-based, research-linked training.

Core Phrase Bank

Tap a card to reveal how to use it. Have her repeat and personalize each one out loud immediately, plugging in a real Ghent fact from the previous tab.
What draws me to Ghent University specifically is...▾
Use for "why this university / why here" — always follow with one concrete detail, not a general compliment.
"What draws me to Ghent University specifically is the way dentistry sits inside a larger, research-driven Faculty of Medicine and Health Sciences."
What appeals to me about this position is the combination of...▾
Use to show she understands this is a dual role (research + teaching), not just a PhD.
"What appeals to me about this position is the combination of carrying out my own research and teaching students who are just starting out — I want both, not just one."
I've always found that teaching something helps me...▾
Grammar: present perfect (have/has + past participle) — links a pattern from her past to who she is now, rather than reporting one finished event.
"I've always found that teaching something helps me understand it more deeply myself."
One thing I respect about Ghent's approach is...▾
Use to compliment the institution with substance — plug in a real fact (evidence-based training, international student body, research culture).
"One thing I respect about Ghent's approach is how clinically and evidence-based the postgraduate dental training is."
If my research and teaching schedules ever conflicted, I would...▾
Grammar: second conditional — for the "how would you balance research and teaching" curveball.
"If my research and teaching schedules ever conflicted, I would prioritize by deadline and communicate early with my supervisor."
As someone who moves between English and Dutch daily, I...▾
Use to turn her bilingual/multilingual daily reality into a strength, fitting Ghent's international, multilingual student body.
"As someone who moves between English and Dutch daily, I'm used to adapting how I explain things depending on who's in front of me — which matters when teaching a mixed classroom."

The Answer Frame: Claim → Reason → Example

Same shape as before, now loaded with Ghent-specific content instead of generic material.
1
Claim
Say your main point in one short sentence.
2
Reason
Explain why, in one sentence.
3
Example
Give one short, concrete detail — ideally something specific to Ghent.
Grammar focus — second conditional for "how would you handle..." questions
If + past simple, ... + would + base verb
"If research and teaching ever competed for my time, I would plan the week around fixed deadlines first."
"If I didn't know how to answer a student's question, I would say so honestly and follow up."
Worked example — "Why Ghent University?"
Claim: "I want to do my PhD at Ghent because it fits both what I want to research and how I want to grow as a teacher."
Reason: "The Faculty of Medicine and Health Sciences puts dentistry inside a much larger, evidence-based research environment, which is exactly the kind of rigor I want around me."
Example: "For example, the postgraduate program in periodontology and implantology is internationally accredited and clinically oriented — that combination of research and real clinical practice is rare."
Worked example — "Why do you want to combine research and teaching?"
Claim: "I want this role specifically because it's both, not one or the other."
Reason: "Teaching forces me to explain ideas simply, which sharpens my own understanding for the research side."
Example: "For example, when I've explained a clinical concept to someone less experienced, I've often noticed gaps in my own reasoning that I then had to fix."
