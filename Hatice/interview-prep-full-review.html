The Three Structures That Carry Every Answer

Not new material — a fast refresh of the grammar that's shown up in every session.

1 · Second conditional — for "what would you do if..."

Formula
If + past simple, ... + would + base verb
"If a patient was/were anxious, I would explain each step slowly."
"If my research and teaching schedules ever conflicted, I would prioritize by deadline."

2 · Present perfect vs past simple

The difference
Present perfect (have/has + past participle) links a pattern from the past to who she is now. Past simple reports one finished event, done and closed.
Pattern → now: "I've always found that teaching something helps me understand it more deeply."
One finished event: "Last year, I decided to apply for this position."

3 · Sequential linkers — for connected stories

while later after that as soon as finally
Model — the shape of a good story
"While I was explaining the procedure, she suddenly said she wanted to stop. So I stopped straight away. Later, once she felt calmer, I asked if she wanted to try again. After that, she agreed to a full check-up. As soon as it was finished, she looked relieved. Finally, she booked a follow-up herself."
