A Full Narrative, Step by Step

Interviewers often ask: "Tell me about a time you handled a difficult situation at work." A strong answer isn't a list of facts — it's a connected story with background, a complication, a resolution, and a point. Listen to this model, then notice the linkers holding it together.
Background A few months ago, I had a patient who was terrified of the dentist. She hadn't been for an examination in over ten years, and she was shaking before I'd even touched anything.
Complication While I was explaining the procedure, she suddenly said she wanted to stop and leave. I could see she was completely overwhelmed.
Resolution So I stopped straight away and just talked to her for a few minutes — no equipment, no pressure. Later, once she felt calmer, I asked if she wanted to try again, just for one minute. After that, she agreed to a full check-up. As soon as it was finished, she looked relieved rather than anxious. Finally, she booked a follow-up appointment herself — something she said she hadn't done in a decade.
Point That experience taught me that patience is often more effective than persuasion — and it's a big part of why I believe trust matters as much as technical skill in this job.

The Linkers You'll Use

These sequential linkers move a listener through time without you needing to say "and then" five times in a row.
later after that finally while as soon as
Read the model aloud with real pacing/pauses so the structure is audible, not just visible. Elicit the four stages (background / complication / resolution / point) from her before naming them, if time allows. Flag the past simple + past continuous weave ("while I was explaining... she suddenly said") — this is the grammar backbone of B1-01.

Reorder the Story

These sentences are from another STAR-structured story — a mix-up during a busy afternoon at the clinic. Tap them in the order you think they happened.
  • As soon as I checked the schedule, I realized two patients had been booked for the same time slot.
  • It was a Friday afternoon and the clinic was fully booked, back to back, with no gaps.
  • Finally, both patients left satisfied, and neither of them even realized there had been a mix-up.
  • While I was finishing up with one patient, the receptionist came in looking stressed.
  • After that, I asked the receptionist to explain the situation calmly to one patient while I saw the other first.
